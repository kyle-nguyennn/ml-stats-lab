## Q1(a) Multilinear algebra

Given

\[
A=
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 2\\
3 & 4
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
B=
\begin{bmatrix}
5 & 6\\
7 & 8
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

### (i) Kronecker product \(A\otimes B\)

Using the block definition,

\[
A\otimes B
=
\begin{bmatrix}
1B & 2B\\
3B & 4B
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
5 & 6 & 10 & 12\\
7 & 8 & 14 & 16\\
15 & 18 & 20 & 24\\
21 & 24 & 28 & 32
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

### (ii) Khatri-Rao product \(A\odot B\)

The Khatri-Rao product is the matching-column Kronecker product:

\[
A\odot B
=
\begin{bmatrix}
a_1\otimes b_1 & a_2\otimes b_2
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Thus,

\[
a_1\otimes b_1
=
\begin{bmatrix}
1\\3
\end{bmatrix}
\otimes
\begin{bmatrix}
5\\7
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
5\\7\\15\\21
\end{bmatrix},
\]

and

\[
a_2\otimes b_2
=
\begin{bmatrix}
2\\4
\end{bmatrix}
\otimes
\begin{bmatrix}
6\\8
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
12\\16\\24\\32
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Therefore,

\[
A\odot B
=
\begin{bmatrix}
5 & 12\\
7 & 16\\
15 & 24\\
21 & 32
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

### (iii) Hadamard product \(A*B\)

Elementwise multiplication gives

\[
A*B
=
\begin{bmatrix}
1\cdot 5 & 2\cdot 6\\
3\cdot 7 & 4\cdot 8
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
5 & 12\\
21 & 32
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

### (iv) \((A\otimes B)^{-1}\)

Starting from

\[
\left[
\begin{array}{cccc|cccc}
5&6&10&12&1&0&0&0\\
7&8&14&16&0&1&0&0\\
15&18&20&24&0&0&1&0\\
21&24&28&32&0&0&0&1
\end{array}
\right],
\]

row reduction gives the upper-triangular form

\[
\left[
\begin{array}{cccc|cccc}
1&0&-12&-16&-4&0&0&1\\
0&8&98&128&28&1&0&-7\\
0&0&10&12&3&0&-1&0\\
0&0&0&4&21&-15&-7&5
\end{array}
\right].
\]

Now eliminate the fourth column:

\[
R_1\leftarrow R_1+4R_4,
\qquad
R_3\leftarrow R_3-3R_4,
\qquad
R_2\leftarrow R_2-32R_4,
\]

which gives

\[
\left[
\begin{array}{cccc|cccc}
1&0&-12&0&80&-60&-28&21\\
0&8&98&0&-644&481&224&-167\\
0&0&10&0&-60&45&20&-15\\
0&0&0&4&21&-15&-7&5
\end{array}
\right].
\]

Normalize the last two pivots:

\[
R_3\leftarrow \frac1{10}R_3,
\qquad
R_4\leftarrow \frac14R_4.
\]

Then

\[
R_2\leftarrow R_2-98R_3,
\qquad
R_2\leftarrow \frac18R_2,
\qquad
R_1\leftarrow R_1+12R_3.
\]

Therefore,

\[
(A\otimes B)^{-1}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
8&-6&-4&3\\
-7&5&\frac72&-\frac52\\
-6&\frac92&2&-\frac32\\
\frac{21}{4}&-\frac{15}{4}&-\frac74&\frac54
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

### (v) \(A^{-1}\otimes B^{-1}\)

First,

\[
A^{-1}
=
\frac{1}{1\cdot4-2\cdot3}
\begin{bmatrix}
4&-2\\
-3&1
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
-2&1\\
\frac32&-\frac12
\end{bmatrix},
\]

and

\[
B^{-1}
=
\frac{1}{5\cdot8-6\cdot7}
\begin{bmatrix}
8&-6\\
-7&5
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
-4&3\\
\frac72&-\frac52
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Hence

\[
A^{-1}\otimes B^{-1}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
8&-6&-4&3\\
-7&5&\frac72&-\frac52\\
-6&\frac92&2&-\frac32\\
\frac{21}{4}&-\frac{15}{4}&-\frac74&\frac54
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Thus,

\[
\boxed{(A\otimes B)^{-1}=A^{-1}\otimes B^{-1}}.
\]

## Q1(b)

Let

\[
A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n},\quad
B\in\mathbb R^{r\times s},\quad
C\in\mathbb R^{n\times p},\quad
D\in\mathbb R^{s\times t}.
\]

We want to show

\[
(A\otimes B)(C\otimes D)=AC\otimes BD.
\]

Using the block representation,

\[
A\otimes B
=
\begin{bmatrix}
a_{11}B & a_{12}B & \cdots & a_{1n}B\\
a_{21}B & a_{22}B & \cdots & a_{2n}B\\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\
a_{m1}B & a_{m2}B & \cdots & a_{mn}B
\end{bmatrix},
\]

and

\[
C\otimes D
=
\begin{bmatrix}
c_{11}D & \cdots & c_{1p}D\\
c_{21}D & \cdots & c_{2p}D\\
\vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\
c_{n1}D & \cdots & c_{np}D
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

The \((i,j)\)-th block of the product is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\big[(A\otimes B)(C\otimes D)\big]_{ij}
&=
\sum_{k=1}^{n}(a_{ik}B)(c_{kj}D)\\
&=
\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{ik}c_{kj}BD\\
&=
\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{ik}c_{kj}\right)BD\\
&=
(AC)_{ij}BD.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is exactly the \((i,j)\)-th block of \(AC\otimes BD\). Hence

\[
\boxed{(A\otimes B)(C\otimes D)=AC\otimes BD}.
\]

The dimensions also agree:

\[
A\otimes B\in\mathbb R^{mr\times ns},
\qquad
C\otimes D\in\mathbb R^{ns\times pt},
\]

so the product lies in \(\mathbb R^{mr\times pt}\).

## Q1(c)

Let

\[
A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n},\quad
B\in\mathbb R^{p\times q},\quad
X\in\mathbb R^{q\times n},\quad
Y\in\mathbb R^{p\times m}.
\]

We want to show

\[
\operatorname{vec}(Y)^\top(A\otimes B)\operatorname{vec}(X)
=
\operatorname{tr}(A^\top Y^\top B X).
\]

Use the identities

\[
\operatorname{vec}(AXB)
=
(B^\top\otimes A)\operatorname{vec}(X),
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{vec}(U)^\top\operatorname{vec}(V)
=
\operatorname{tr}(U^\top V).
\]

Choosing the left matrix as \(B\) and the right matrix as \(A^\top\),

\[
(A\otimes B)\operatorname{vec}(X)
=
\operatorname{vec}(BXA^\top).
\]

Therefore,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{vec}(Y)^\top(A\otimes B)\operatorname{vec}(X)
&=
\operatorname{vec}(Y)^\top\operatorname{vec}(BXA^\top)\\
&=
\operatorname{tr}(Y^\top BXA^\top).
\end{aligned}
\]

Using the cyclic property of the trace,

\[
\operatorname{tr}(Y^\top BXA^\top)
=
\operatorname{tr}(A^\top Y^\top BX).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{vec}(Y)^\top(A\otimes B)\operatorname{vec}(X)
=
\operatorname{tr}(A^\top Y^\top BX)
}.
\]
